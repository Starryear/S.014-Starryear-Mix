# Starryear Three-Photo Scroll Full Prompt

Treat exactly three related Buddhist user photographs as immutable evidence and as material for two visual translations. Create one vertical 2:3 editorial master sheet: the three originals at upper-left, an absurd and surreal reconstruction at upper-right, a landscape 16:9 deconstruction below, and a compact editorial signature in the central negative-space seam: one minimalist logo assembled from three source-derived forms and one short piece of artistic French. Do not make an ordinary photo grid, isolate the signature in a generic title strip, default to a red-blue palette, rely on fixed geometric templates, or invent locations, scriptures, or historical facts.

## 1. Input and internal analysis

Assign anchor, environment, and structural-detail roles. Internally inspect subject contours, spatial direction, shadows, perspective, negative space, light, weather, motion, atmosphere, material, color temperature, and crop boundaries. All three originals must appear in the evidence cluster and contribute traceable but non-repeating material to both translations.

Extract a fresh working palette of two or three colors from the three source photographs every time. Choose hues from dominant illumination, materials, environment, or a meaningful contrast genuinely present in the images. Do not default to warm ivory, red and blue, or reuse the palette of a previous work. Keep color disciplined; avoid rainbow, neon, candy palettes, complex gradients, and evenly distributed multicolor schemes.

## 2. Upper heavy reconstruction

The upper-right hero is responsible for absurdity and surreal reconstruction. Establish one immediately legible impossible event. When Buddhist imagery is present, enlarge the Buddha head to roughly 2–5 times its expected scale so it behaves like a mountain, moon, horizon, gateway, or suspended architectural mass, while people, buildings, clouds, or landscape remain tiny and perspectivally believable. The head may cross boundaries, float, interlock with architecture, or replace the horizon. Exaggerate scale and spatial role, never facial anatomy, expression, mudra, or defining iconography.

Build the absurdity around one dominant contradiction and one quieter echo—for example a colossal Buddha head against a miniature documentary world, an interior becoming a valley, architecture passing through a halo, or gravity visibly reversing. Do not pile up unrelated creatures or decorative dream symbols. Fuse the sources through reliable isolation, extreme scale jumps, overlap, cross-layer continuity, and two to four large flat color fields.

Derive every field contour from the photographs: a subject or object silhouette, shadow edge, architectural perspective, aperture, reflection, occlusion boundary, or negative space. These contours may be simplified, enlarged, cropped, or extended, but do not begin from a reusable library of discs, arches, or wedges. Fields must behave as spatial structure, not decorative stickers.

Cropping, overlap, extreme scaling, selective monochrome, local color, slight perspective correction, and edge treatment are allowed. Preserve identity, faces, hands, objects, existing inscriptions, religious imagery, and defining architecture; monumental does not mean deformed. Add no text inside the hero.

## 3. Lower release and deconstruction

Create a landscape 16:9 work below that releases elements, crops, silhouettes, field contours, and spatial relationships. The sources may become three to five recognizable photographic fragments, displaced rhythm, negative space, scale jumps, contour echoes, and material excerpts.

It must decisively differ from the upper reconstruction: do not repeat its element selection, crop logic, silhouettes, color-field contours, composition, center of gravity, or area balance. The two stages share only their sources and working palette, not a ready-made layout. Never obtain the lower work by merely cropping, stretching, rotating, duplicating, or recoloring the upper one.

## 4. Final master sheet

Build a vertical 2:3 canvas, adaptable within a 0.64–0.68 width-to-height ratio. Choose a restrained light or neutral ground from the photographs' atmosphere and working palette; warm ivory is not mandatory.

- Upper section: about 60%–64% of total height.
- Upper-left: about 30%–34% of total width, holding all three source photos as one larger image plus two staggered smaller images, without generative filtering.
- Upper-right: about 54%–59% of total width, holding the heavy reconstruction as the largest field.
- Between or partly across them: preserve about 8%–12% of the width as source-derived negative space or a quiet tonal transition for the logo-and-French editorial signature; do not leave it as an empty white stripe or turn it into a rigid title column.
- Lower section: about 36%–40% of total height, with the landscape deconstruction spanning nearly the full width.
- Keep outer margins and gutters coherent; avoid edge crowding, black frames, ordinary equal cards, and screenshot appearance.

Crop and scale source evidence only. Minor exposure and white-balance correction is allowed, but do not redraw or alter facts. Apply the derived palette and shape system to translated fields and editorial space without covering or recoloring the source evidence.

## 5. Three-part logo, artistic French, and integration

Extract one different simplified form from each source photograph: a subject contour, architectural angle, halo arc, aperture, shadow, object line, or negative shape. Fuse the three through a shared axis, interlock, overlap, or continuous gesture into one compact minimalist logo. At small size it must read as one mark, not three adjacent icons, a stock geometric trademark, a monogram, a religious seal, or a watermark. Do not reduce a Buddha face, mudra, sacred text, ritual object, or defining religious attribute into decoration.

Pair the logo with one concise, natural, poetic but legible French phrase. For a Buddha-birth theme, default to `L'ÉVEIL NAISSANT`, conveying “the awakening being born.” If the user provides another intended meaning, translate it into idiomatic French rather than literally word by word. Every newly added word must be French—no Sanskrit, Chinese, English, romanization, dates, or secondary caption. Do not present it as scripture, a historical quotation, or a doctrinal translation.

Treat the logo and phrase as one integrated editorial signature within the central seam. They may stack, sit side by side, or lightly interlock, together occupying roughly 60%–80% of the seam's usable height while retaining breathing room. Favor elegant high-contrast serif, restrained custom lettering, or spare hand-drawn forms with deliberate tracking and scale rhythm. Match color, edge softness, ink density, and texture to the surrounding image; use black, deep brown, or a dark working-palette hue. Allow subtle baseline shifts, flying-white gaps, dry-brush drag, broken ink, and handmade irregularity, but preserve French accents, apostrophes, spelling, and legibility. If the image model cannot render the phrase reliably, generate the art and logo without text, typeset the exact French in post-production, and integrate it with masks and sampled texture. Existing text inside an untouched source photograph remains evidence and must not be changed.

## 6. Material and boundaries

Allow restrained paper fibers, fine grain, local halftone, torn edges, dry brush, or light mist only when they answer this image set; do not impose a fixed vintage filter. Style references govern editorial density and compositional principles only. Never borrow their subjects, motifs, texture placement, brush marks, or exact composition. Avoid strong vignettes, neon glow, fake HDR, plastic smoothing, excessive sharpening, compression noise, and jagged masks.

## 7. Output

Return exactly one finished vertical master-sheet bitmap. Do not return separate stages, analysis, alternatives, prompt explanations, phone previews, frames, watermarks, or extra notes. The central three-part logo and artistic French must appear inside this single final artwork. The result must clearly read as source evidence → upper surreal reconstruction through absurd scale → lower release and deconstruction, with no repeated translation design between upper and lower fields. If the surreal intensity is weak, enlarge the main Buddha head further, reduce the scale of documentary reference figures, and strengthen occlusion and horizon relationships instead of adding decoration.
