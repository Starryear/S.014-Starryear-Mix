# Starryear 三图成卷

Build one finished vertical editorial sheet from exactly three user photographs. Preserve the three sources as a visible evidence group, derive a source-specific surreal reconstruction and a distinct horizontal deconstruction, then fill the central negative-space seam with one compact editorial signature: a three-part minimalist logo paired with concise artistic French. The sheet must move from evidence through impossible scale into release. Its palette, shapes, movement, logo geometry, and lettering placement must be freshly extracted from the photographs rather than selected from fixed defaults.

## Workflow

1. Inspect the three photographs and assign anchor, environment, and structural-detail roles by visual strength. Record any location text explicitly supplied by the user; never infer a place name from appearance alone.
2. Extract a working palette of two or three colors from the photographs themselves. Choose colors that express the dominant light, material, atmosphere, or a meaningful contrast present in the sources; do not default to red and blue.
3. Create the upper hero collage first as a portrait-oriented, source-faithful surreal reconstruction. Establish one dominant impossible event: for Buddhist imagery, the Buddha head may become monumental—roughly 2–5 times the expected scale—while tiny people, architecture, clouds, or landscape retain believable perspective. Let the head behave like a mountain, moon, horizon, gateway, or suspended architectural mass. Preserve the face and iconographic identity; exaggerate scale and spatial role, not anatomy or expression.
4. Create the lower landscape 16:9 deconstruction second. Release the sources into fragments, crops, silhouettes, and source-derived color-field contours. It must use a different composition, area balance, element selection, crop logic, silhouette language, and color-block outline from the upper reconstruction.
5. Assemble a single vertical 2:3 master sheet. Place three minimally edited source photographs as a compact evidence cluster in the upper-left, the heavy reconstruction as the large upper-right field, and the landscape deconstruction across the full lower band. Preserve a source-derived central negative-space seam or quiet tonal transition, then use it deliberately for the logo-and-French signature rather than leaving it visually unfinished.
6. Design one small minimalist logo from exactly three abstract components, one traceable to each source photograph. Reduce each source to a distinct contour, stroke, aperture, halo fragment, architectural angle, or silhouette; join the three into one coherent compact mark with shared alignment or overlap. It must read as a single symbol at small size, not three icons, a religious seal, a corporate trademark, a monogram, or generic stock geometry. Preserve respectful abstraction: do not reduce a Buddha face, mudra, sacred script, or defining religious attribute into a decorative logo.
7. Pair the logo with one concise piece of artistic French. For a Buddha-birth theme, default to `L'ÉVEIL NAISSANT` (“the awakening being born”); if the user supplies another intended meaning, render it as natural, idiomatic French rather than word-for-word translation. Use French for every newly added word in the artwork—no Sanskrit, Chinese, English, romanization, dates, or secondary captions. Keep the phrase short enough to function visually and do not present it as scripture, a historical quotation, or a doctrinal translation.
8. Treat the logo and French phrase as one image-bearing editorial signature inside the central seam. The logo may sit above, beside, or lightly interlock with the words; together they should occupy roughly 60%–80% of the seam's usable height while retaining breathing room. Favor elegant high-contrast serif, restrained custom lettering, or spare hand-drawn letterforms with considered tracking. Allow subtle baseline shifts, scale contrast, dry-brush drag, broken ink, or handmade irregularity, but preserve accents, apostrophes, spelling, and legibility. Match color, edge softness, ink density, and texture to the surrounding composition. If the image model cannot render French reliably, generate the art and logo without text, then typeset the exact French in post-production and blend it with masks and sampled texture.
9. Return exactly one flattened final sheet. Intermediate images may be produced internally but are not additional deliverables unless requested.

## Guardrails

- All three source photographs must appear unchanged in content in the evidence cluster; crop and scale only, with no generative redrawing.
- Do not fabricate or “correct” a city, temple, monument, country, date, attribution, quotation, inscription, scripture, or historical claim.
- Preserve cultural, religious, architectural, and human identity. Monumental scaling, levitation, impossible adjacency, and spatial paradox are encouraged in the translated fields, but do not caricature the Buddha's facial anatomy, mudra, expression, or defining iconography.
- The upper and lower works must share source DNA but cannot repeat element selection, crop pattern, silhouette, color-field contour, or composition; do not merely crop, rotate, or rebalance the same design.
- Build absurdity through one legible spatial contradiction, not random decoration: giant sacred head versus tiny documentary world, interior becoming landscape, architecture passing through a halo, or gravity visibly reversing. Prefer one strong impossible event plus one quieter secondary echo.
- Use supplied style references only for palette and large-shape logic. Do not copy their exact subjects, flames, temples, lotus motifs, paper tears, halftone placement, brush marks, or compositions.
- Prefer two to four large, clean fields whose shapes can be traced to the photographs' contours, shadows, perspective, or negative space. Avoid a reusable library of circles, arches, wedges, gradients, rainbow palettes, equal polka dots, infographic cards, and fields that obscure defining source details.
- Every newly added word in the artwork must be idiomatic French; do not place Sanskrit, Chinese, English, romanization, translations, dates, secondary captions, watermarks, phone UI, or extra typography. Existing text visible inside an untouched source photograph remains part of the evidence and must not be altered.
- The three-part logo and French phrase must feel embedded in the central negative space rather than pasted into a white box, rigid title strip, brand lockup, or isolated label. Integration may affect placement and texture, never spelling, accents, or legibility.
- Avoid ordinary grids, equal card layouts, screenshot chrome, muddy filters, fake HDR, plastic smoothing, excessive grain, and unrelated decorative objects.
